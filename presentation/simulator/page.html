Remote inference under network faults

DRTC Fault Simulator

A robot arm plays a chunk of actions (a batch of planned moves, played at 30 Hz) while a GPU server computes the next chunk. This is a simplified model of three robot clients when the network fails: LeRobot as shipped, LeRobot made patient, and Distributed Real-Time Chunking (DRTC). Press Replay, then use the orange buttons to break the network while it plays.

What the three clients do

  • LeRobot as shipped asks for the next chunk when about half a second of actions is left and waits for the reply. If the queue stays empty for 1 s, or a reply is 5 s late, it ends the run.
  • LeRobot, patient is the same client with those two limits raised to the whole 14 s trial. When the queue runs dry it cancels the request in flight and sends one fresh request. If that one is lost too, nothing else is sent, and LeRobot's third limit (no action for 10 s after a restart) ends the run.
  • DRTC has three parts. The early trigger asks for the next chunk when H − smin actions are left (H: the chunk length; smin: 20 actions, 8 for the short-chunk policies). The latency estimator tracks the round trip and its variation, as TCP does. The cooldown re-request gives up a request once it is overdue by that estimate and sends a fresh observation. Untick a part to see what it adds.

Try this

  1. Keep SmolVLA, choose Outage and press Replay. LeRobot as shipped ends the run; DRTC moves again within about a second of the outage ending.
  2. Choose GR00T N1.5 with no faults. Its 16-action chunk lasts 0.53 s, about as long as its 0.55 s round trip (network plus inference), so at the default delay every client stalls without any fault.
  3. Go back to SmolVLA with Outage and untick Cooldown re-request. The outage swallows DRTC's request, and without the cooldown it waits for its queue to run dry before asking again: the stall grows from about 8% to about 35% of the trial.

How close is this to the real thing?

The scheduling rules are taken from the project's client code and LeRobot 0.7. The rest is simplified: one server that computes one request at a time, a fixed network delay, and the bench's fault shapes. There is no arm, camera or task success in this model. Under spike + drops + outage, its stall times come out close to those measured on the replay bench, for DRTC and for the patient client. The patient client's bench results arrived after this model was built, so that column is a prediction.
